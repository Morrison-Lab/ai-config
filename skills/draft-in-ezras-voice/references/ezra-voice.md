# Ezra's voice: traits for drafting

Learned from Ezra's own typed directives (2026-09 to 2026-11).
Edit this file whenever he corrects a draft.
Keep it free of FERPA data and of agent-written text.

## Traits

- **Direct and short.**
  He states the point first, in a short sentence.
  Example: "cai: always imitate my writing style when drafting communications for me".
- **Imperative for requests.**
  "Fix the failure."
  "Never skip a check."
  "Install whatever is missing."
- **Plain words.**
  "Use", not "utilize".
  "Before", not "prior to".
  No filler openers.
- **Literal language.**
  No idioms ("meet", "come across as") and no metaphors.
- **One idea per sentence, one sentence per line** in source files.
- **Reasons given briefly, in a following sentence**, not in a long subordinate clause.
- **Dates and attribution for decisions.**
  "(Ezra, 2026-10-16)" style.
- **Lists for three or more parallel items**, with a blank line before the list.
- **No hedging or pleasantries.**
  No "I hope this finds you well".
  No "just wanted to".
- **Serial comma.**
  A comma before the final "and" or "or" of three or more items.

## Canvas announcements to students

Learned from nine of his own announcements (2026-10-19 to 2026-11-05), plus his edit of one draft (2026-11-11), which each dated trait names.
The sources are his bodies and edits only, never replies, student posts, or student data.

- **Opening.**
  "Hi all," by default, "Hi folks," or "Hi again," for a same-day follow-up.
  The shortest notices skip the greeting.
- **Sign-off.**
  "Best," then a blank line, then "Ezra".
  "Thanks," then "Ezra" when he asks for something.
  First name only.
  Never "Dear", "Regards", or "Sincerely".
- **Length.**
  Usually 2 to 5 sentences.
  Schedule changes run longer, about 1,200 characters.
- **Structure.**
  Plain paragraphs, with no headings, bold, or bullet lists.
  A list of items becomes one short sentence or paragraph per item.
- **Order.**
  The reason first, then the changes, then what is not changing, then where to ask questions.
- **What's new, not what was broken.**
  An announcement about updated materials leads with what is new or useful for students, such as new figures, an HTML version or a note sheet.
  It leaves out defects already fixed, such as code that leaked into a PDF or a corrected typo.
  Ezra wrote, editing a Midterm 2 practice-materials draft (2026-11-11): "don't mention this issue".
  He added: "the new figures and html version and the note sheet are what's important".
- **Dates.**
  Weekday first, then the date in parentheses with an abbreviated month and no ordinal: "Wednesday (Nov 4)".
- **Names.**
  "HW 3" with a space.
  Informal titles are lowercase ("midterm 1 postponed").
- **Links.**
  A bare URL.
- **Closer.**
  Points to a channel or action: "Please post questions in Discussions!"
- **Tone.**
  Warm and informal, in first person, with "we" for the class.
  Mildly apologetic about logistics, never grovelling.
  Light human touches ("Happy Halloween to those who celebrate!").
- **Punctuation.**
  Semicolons and parentheses for asides.
  Exclamation marks on warm closers and offers, never on bad news.
  No em dashes.
